## 1. Retail/e-commerce (simulation/supplement variant) — where should the sales manager intervene?

**Stakeholder brief and decision.** The sales manager must choose a channel or customer segment for next month's investigation and a discount policy experiment. Revenue growth can hide weaker gross margin or returns. She needs a qualified recommendation with a metric definition and uncertainty, rather than a ranking alone. This is the deeply guided introductory case; keep advanced refund modeling outside its first two-hour pilot.

**Business questions.** How do monthly discounted sales, orders and AOV change? Which channels combine volume and gross profit? Are higher discounts associated with lower margins? How do observed returns change the assessment of a mature sale cohort? Do customer and order counts answer different questions?

**Data and dictionary.** Core `data/prepared/v1.0.0/retail_order_lines.csv`: one completed 2025 order line, key `(order_id,line_id)`. Multiple lines share one order; `order_id` is not unique. Order date, channel and region belong to the order; product category belongs to line. Source challenge uses `orders.csv`, `order_lines.csv`, `products.csv` and `return_events.csv`. Advanced prepared order summary is one order, with separate refund-event table. Dictionary must explain positive quantity, price per unit, discount **per line**, positive line COGS, fictional customer identity and no tax/freight/overhead. Required versioned dictionary: `data/metadata/v1.0.0/retail_dictionary.md`.

**Metric contract.** Gross sales = quantity × unit price; discounted sales = gross sales − line discount; gross profit = discounted sales − COGS. AOV = SUM(discounted sales)/COUNTD(order); margin = SUM(gross profit)/SUM(discounted sales). Pre-return figures are labeled “before refunds.” Use order-level dimensions for core AOV comparisons. Zero denominator → N/A; missing monetary values block clean release. Canceled orders excluded. Monthly growth compares adjacent months; January has no baseline. Advanced return incidence counts distinct returned orders / distinct sold orders; refund-adjusted sale cohorts use refunds observed through cutoff and are not calendar-period accounting revenue. Return-event-date totals and order-month cohort totals must remain distinct. Recent cohorts have less follow-up.

**Observable outcome and portfolio evidence.** Learner explains a row, demonstrates why line count differs from order count, produces checked weighted AOV/margin, and supports a channel recommendation with two views and a validation note. Evidence: an individual Tableau workbook/export, metric card, and 150–250-word business note. Learner-facing pilot artifact is planned at `docs/lessons/03-retail-metrics.md`.

**Demonstration / guided worked example.** Instructor starts from the decision and inspects a tiny deliberately constructed two-order, multi-line fixture. Class predicts correct distinct order count and AOV by hand, then checks Tableau. Create one monthly trend and one channel comparison; reconcile overall sales with source totals. Fixture amounts and final answers are to be computed after the dataset release, not invented now. Full detailed pacing is specified separately in `planning/PILOT_LESSON.md`.

**Independent practice.** Reproduce channel AOV and margin; compare two months using explicit filters; contrast customer count and order count; write a cautious explanation of an observed discount/margin association. Practice sheet: `docs/cases/retail/exercises.md`.

**Debugging.** Repair a workbook using `COUNT(order_id)` as orders, mean of row margins as company margin, and category order counts summed as company orders. Explain the wrong denominator, not only the corrected formula. Advanced option diagnoses a multi-event returns join multiplying original sales. Planned task: `docs/cases/retail/debugging.md`.

**Recommendation challenge.** Allocate a hypothetical limited investigation effort to one channel. Include margin, volume and sample size; propose what evidence would justify changing discounts. Avoid causal conclusions from association and acknowledge return follow-up. Planned prompt: `docs/cases/retail/challenge.md`.

**Optional Python connection.** `notebooks/01_retail_companion.ipynb`: `nunique`, `groupby`, additive totals versus ratios of totals, many-to-one merge validation, and separately aggregated refunds. Coding is not needed to earn core outcome credit.

**Validation and instructor separation.** Check line-key uniqueness, one channel per order, arithmetic identities, valid dates, all canceled orders removed, distinct order reconciliation and zero-denominator display. Test category nonadditivity and refund limits; compare workbook totals/filters against the exact released answer file. Future instructor artifacts: `instructor/solutions/v1.0.0/retail_answers.csv`, `instructor/retail_notes.md`, and `instructor/solutions/v1.0.0/retail_reference.twbx`. Notes contain demonstration script, likely misconceptions, recovery steps, challenge alternatives and evidence rubric. No numerical answers or workbook are yet verified.

## 2. Finance (simulation/supplement variant) — which spending variance needs attention?

**Stakeholder brief and decision.** The finance partner must prioritize department/account exceptions for a monthly expense review. A large absolute variance can be expected with a larger budget; a seemingly favorable credit may merely reverse an earlier posting. This case builds reconciliation and sign interpretation before persuasive dashboarding.

**Business questions.** Where are absolute and percentage budget variances material? What is the effect of credits and timing? Which actuals have no known budget? Does the total reconcile to the signed transaction source and unique budget cells?

**Data and dictionary.** Beginner `finance_monthly.csv` grain one `(month,department_id,account_id)` after independent aggregation. Source challenge uses posting-grain `finance_actuals.csv` and unique-cell `finance_budget.csv`. Dictionary: `data/metadata/v1.0.0/finance_dictionary.md`, including account/department labels, posting date versus month, NPR, positive expense/negative credit signs, and missing budget versus explicit zero budget. No audit, statutory, profit-and-loss completeness, or retail-revenue reconciliation is claimed.

**Metric contract.** Scope all 2025 management-expense postings. Actual = sum signed posting amounts; budget = sum unique approved cells. Variance = actual − budget: positive unfavorable for expense, negative favorable. Variance percentage divides by positive budget only. Zero budget → N/A percent with explicit exception label; unknown budget → unknown variance. Missing actual becomes zero only after posting completeness is established. Never average percentages to obtain a total variance percentage; recompute from totals for comparable known-budget scope and show excluded unknown-budget actuals.

**Observable outcome and evidence.** Learner reconciles a monthly comparison table, diagnoses a duplicated budget, and names the largest credible expense exception with correct favorability. Submit a department/account variance view, reconciliation note, and a short recommendation. Core lesson outline path: `docs/cases/finance/index.md`.

**Demonstration / guided worked example.** Predict what happens when one monthly budget joins to three postings. Show budget repeated three times, then aggregate actuals to month-department-account and validate budget uniqueness before combining. Compare two expense cells with a credit and different budget sizes. Use explicit hand fixture first, exact release second; both answer sets are future work.

**Independent practice.** Create amount and percentage exception views; identify an actual-only and a budget-only cell; explain why a credit changes actual spending but does not prove operating improvement. Planned sheet: `docs/cases/finance/exercises.md`.

**Debugging.** Repair duplicated budget totals, an inner join that drops unbudgeted actuals, and reversed expense-favorability colors. Show before/after reconciliation evidence. Planned task: `docs/cases/finance/debugging.md`.

**Recommendation challenge.** Select three review priorities using amount, relative variance, data quality, and potential reversals; propose follow-up questions and avoid claiming all favorable underspending is good performance. Planned prompt: `docs/cases/finance/challenge.md`.

**Optional Python connection.** `notebooks/02_finance_companion.ipynb`: aggregate transactions, validate budget key uniqueness, outer-merge one-to-one, distinguish missing from zero, and reconcile signed totals.

**Validation and instructor separation.** Check posting IDs, budget keys, month mapping, source/post-aggregation signed actual equality, preserved budget total, and full key coverage. Verify known zero, missing budget and negative credit cases. Future `instructor/solutions/v1.0.0/finance_answers.csv`, `instructor/finance_notes.md`, and `instructor/solutions/v1.0.0/finance_reference.twbx` include correct totals, intentional duplication evidence, color/sign recovery, and alternative defensible priorities. ## 3. Operations (simulation/supplement variant) — how should the service manager address delays?

**Stakeholder brief and decision.** The operations manager must choose a carrier or warehouse process to investigate while protecting delivery reliability. Completed deliveries alone can make a severe pending backlog disappear. This case introduces event dates, cutoff, incomplete outcomes and distributions.

**Business questions.** What fraction of shipments due by cutoff arrived on time? How does reliability differ across carriers and warehouses? How old is overdue pending work? How do mean and median completed-shipment lead times differ, and what do they omit?

**Data and dictionary.** `operations_shipments.csv` grain one noncanceled dispatched shipment; key `shipment_id`, not `order_id` because orders can split. Source `shipments.csv`. Dictionary `data/metadata/v1.0.0/operations_dictionary.md` defines dispatch, contractual promise, actual delivery, calendar-day units, cutoff and pending status. Delivery null means undelivered by cutoff; unknown promise is a separate quality problem. Not-yet-due shipments remain in the file for workload analysis.

**Metric contract.** Due population: promised date <= 2025-12-31, dispatched by cutoff, noncanceled. On-time = delivered by promised date and cutoff; on-time rate = on-time due shipments / **all due shipments**, including overdue undelivered. Future-due shipments excluded from this denominator even when early-delivered. Missing promise excluded with an explicit count; unexpected missing promise blocks clean release. Completed lead time = delivery − dispatch, mean/median for completed shipments only. Pending days past due = max(cutoff − promise, 0); due today pending is a service-snapshot failure with age zero. Overall rates use shipment counts, not average carrier percentages. All zero denominators return N/A.

**Observable outcome and evidence.** Learner constructs a cutoff-consistent service metric and a backlog view, reconciles statuses, and explains survivor bias in completed-only lead time. Submit carrier comparison, distribution and backlog summary with due counts and a process recommendation. Planned core lesson: `docs/cases/operations/index.md`.

**Demonstration / guided worked example.** Class categorizes a small fixture containing on-time delivered, late delivered, overdue pending and future-due pending rows. Predict numerator and denominator before calculation, then create a due-population service view and completed-only lead-time histogram/box plot. Verify the late tail affects mean more than median.

**Independent practice.** Compare warehouse reliability with carrier mix visible; report median and mean alongside completion count; find the oldest overdue pending shipments; state the effect of unknown promise dates. Planned sheet: `docs/cases/operations/exercises.md`.

**Debugging.** Recover a dashboard whose delivered-only filter removes overdue pending shipments. Diagnose a second error counting split orders rather than shipments and a null date replaced by cutoff that fakes completion. Planned task: `docs/cases/operations/debugging.md`.

**Recommendation challenge.** Prioritize one process investigation with service rate, due volume, backlog age and mix; specify the additional operational evidence needed before blaming a carrier. Planned prompt: `docs/cases/operations/challenge.md`.

**Optional Python connection.** `notebooks/03_operations_companion.ipynb`: parsed dates, boolean populations, null-preserving durations, quantiles and numerator/denominator reconciliation.

**Validation and instructor separation.** Enumerate all statuses at cutoff; check date ordering; prove pending due rows remain denominator members; reconcile due plus future-due plus unknown-promise populations to eligible total. Test end-of-day promise boundary, split order and zero-due carrier. Future `instructor/solutions/v1.0.0/operations_answers.csv`, `instructor/operations_notes.md`, and `instructor/solutions/v1.0.0/operations_reference.twbx` separate completed-duration answers from service answers, document filter recovery, and supply qualified challenge alternatives. Numerical and workbook checks remain pending.

## 4. Marketing (simulation/supplement variant) — where should the next campaign review focus?

**Stakeholder brief and decision.** The marketing lead must recommend a limited test or budget review across paid campaigns. Unequal campaign sizes and attribution rules can reverse apparent winners. The task is to interpret efficiency with limitations, not infer incremental causal impact.

**Business questions.** Which funnel stage constrains each campaign? What are total weighted CTR, session conversion rate, CPA and attributed ROAS? How do rankings change when volume is included? What do returns, product margin, and attribution rules leave unknown?

**Data and dictionary.** `marketing_daily.csv` grain one `(date,campaign_id)` with campaign channel, spend NPR, impressions, clicks, sessions, conversions and attributed revenue. Dictionary `data/metadata/v1.0.0/marketing_dictionary.md` explicitly defines conversion as a purchasing session with one order in the simplified funnel. `campaign_attribution.csv` links each credited order to one campaign under synthetically assigned single-campaign attribution. Counts are disjoint daily activity totals; they are not deduplicated cross-day people. Revenue is pre-return discounted sales; organic/unattributed orders excluded.

**Metric contract.** CTR = total clicks / total impressions; session conversion rate = conversions / sessions; CPC = spend / clicks; CPA = spend / conversions; attributed ROAS = revenue / spend. Calculate ratios of sums in every view; never average campaign rates as company totals. Zero denominator returns N/A, including zero spend. Spend/conversion totals and sample sizes accompany ratios. A conversion-rate numerator must not use orders if multiple orders per purchasing session are allowed in a later release; dictionary changes would require a metric revision.

**Observable outcome and evidence.** Learner explains a weighted-rate reversal, produces a funnel and efficiency view, and frames a testable recommendation while distinguishing attribution from causation. Submit campaign comparison, calculation/reconciliation note and proposed test brief. Planned core: `docs/cases/marketing/index.md`.

**Demonstration / guided worked example.** Use unequal-sized campaigns to compare arithmetic mean of rates with ratio of total conversions to total sessions. Reconcile daily campaign activity; identify a high-ROAS, low-volume campaign and a zero-conversion campaign; explain N/A CPA. Exact fixture and released totals are future artifacts.

**Independent practice.** Compare weekly campaign rates and spend, choose an appropriate funnel denominator, identify zero-denominator cases and draft a comparison with volume visible. Planned sheet: `docs/cases/marketing/exercises.md`.

**Debugging.** Repair a mean-of-percentages KPI and a many-to-many order join multiplying revenue. Recover a dashboard that silently replaces undefined CPA with zero and ranks it best. Planned task: `docs/cases/marketing/debugging.md`.

**Recommendation challenge.** Recommend one bounded campaign experiment and success metric with attribution, returns, cost and sample-size caveats. Do not promise incremental revenue based on attributed ROAS. Planned prompt: `docs/cases/marketing/challenge.md`.

**Optional Python connection.** `notebooks/04_marketing_companion.ipynb`: groupby totals, safe division, weighted comparison, unique-order attribution and revenue reconciliation.

**Validation and instructor separation.** Validate nonnegative counts/spend and funnel ordering, one campaign per attributed order, daily conversion/revenue totals, date alignment and attribution sum <= scoped retail sales. ## 5. HR (simulation/supplement variant) — is workforce change creating a capacity problem?

**Stakeholder brief and decision.** The HR partner must advise which workforce or recruitment issue deserves management follow-up. Historical headcount and job mix matter; current headcount alone cannot explain earlier turnover. All employees and requisitions are synthetic. No protected demographic attributes are needed for this release.

**Business questions.** How do opening and closing headcount reconcile with hires and exits? Which periods show higher turnover relative to the population exposed? How do transfers change departmental counts? Are recruitment delays concentrated by job family or department? What remains unseen when only completed hires are timed?

**Data and dictionary.** Beginner `hr_monthly.csv` is one company-month, and separate `hr_department_monthly.csv` is one department-month; never combine company total rows and department rows in one summable fact table. `hr_employment.csv` is one employment spell; `hr_recruitment.csv` is one requisition. Sources `employees.csv`, `department_history.csv`, `recruitment.csv` include pre-2025 hires for opening population and tenure. Dictionary `data/metadata/v1.0.0/hr_dictionary.md` defines hire as first active date, exit as **first inactive date**, transfer interval boundaries, approximate average headcount, and null exit/acceptance dates. One spell per employee and at most one hire per requisition are explicit release-1 simplifications.

**Metric contract.** For `[month_start,next_month_start)`, opening counts hires before start with exit null or >= start; closing counts hires before next start with exit null or >= next start. Hires/exits use their event dates in that month. Company close = open + hires − exits; department bridge adds transfers in − transfers out. Monthly average headcount = (open+close)/2, an approximation; monthly turnover = exits / that denominator. Annual turnover divides annual exits by mean of the 12 monthly average headcounts, not current headcount or mean of monthly rates. Zero denominator → N/A. Active-at-cutoff tenure excludes leavers and is labeled accordingly. Time-to-fill = acceptance − opening for accepted requisitions; open requisition age remains a separate measure rather than zero duration.

**Observable outcome and evidence.** Learner reconstructs a historical population, checks a monthly bridge, repairs a wrong turnover denominator and gives a fair workforce recommendation with small-group/job-mix limits. Submit monthly bridge, turnover trend, recruitment duration/open-age comparison and decision note. Planned core: `docs/cases/hr/index.md`.

**Demonstration / guided worked example.** Trace a small employee fixture across month start, month end, hire and first-inactive dates. Class predicts opening, hires, exits and closing before using prepared monthly data. Compare historical-average versus current-headcount turnover; add one department transfer to show why its bridge differs from company totals.

**Independent practice.** Reconcile two months, compare turnover using historical denominators and counts, distinguish active tenure from completed service length, and compare completed time-to-fill with pending requisition ages. Flag small departments rather than ranking them without qualification. Planned sheet: `docs/cases/hr/exercises.md`.

**Debugging.** Repair turnover calculated using current headcount, an exit-date boundary off by one day, and pending hires recorded as zero-day recruitment. Optional challenge identifies department transfers misclassified as company hires/exits. Planned task: `docs/cases/hr/debugging.md`.

**Recommendation challenge.** Recommend one capacity or recruitment investigation with headcount, exits, job-family mix and open roles visible. Include plausible alternative explanations and additional evidence; do not infer employee quality, discrimination, or causal effects from these descriptive synthetic patterns. Planned prompt: `docs/cases/hr/challenge.md`.

**Optional Python connection.** `notebooks/05_hr_companion.ipynb`: interval membership, monthly calendar boundaries, temporal department assignment, bridge assertions and null-preserving recruitment dates.

**Validation and instructor separation.** Check pre-year workforce coverage, hire/exit chronology, no overlapping assignment intervals, month-boundary fixtures, all company bridges and department transfer bridges. Verify annual denominator from 12 months, current-active population and pending recruitment age. Future `instructor/solutions/v1.0.0/hr_answers.csv`, `instructor/hr_notes.md`, and `instructor/solutions/v1.0.0/hr_reference.twbx` record boundary truth tables, acceptable comparison caveats and recovery steps. ## Choice extension C: Government — what does one construction-spending series mean?

**Stakeholder / question / outcome.** A planning analyst needs a comparable historical construction-spending briefing. Use inspected **US construction spending** to choose one well-defined series, identify partial years and distinguish monetary amounts from percentage/error/annual-rate records. Learner produces a qualified period comparison rather than summing the entire file. Suggested 30–45-minute choice practice within existing workload.

**Observed data / proposed dictionary.** Raw CSV is 50,208×20, period-category-data type-error type-geography-adjustment grain, with unique `serialid` and documented composite key. Covers 177 months, January 2002–September 2016, geography US. `val` is missing in **33,216** rows; percentage/error blocks are entirely missing. Missing is not zero spending/growth/error. Future `data/metadata/us-construction/<version>/dictionary.md` freezes unit/adjustment/category filters and overlap rules; neither all rows nor all columns form a summable amount table.

**Recommended metric contract.** Beginner series requires **all** filters: `cat_code='XXXX'`, `dt_code='T'`, `et_idx=0`, `geo_code='US'`, `is_adj=0`. It yields **177 unique nonmissing monthly observations**, nominal million US dollars.
