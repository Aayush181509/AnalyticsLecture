Learning roadmap¶

Twelve two-hour classes, grouped into three stages. Each class starts from a business question and ends with a piece of evidence for your portfolio.

This is a proposed sequence

Lesson instructions and data are released only after they have been checked. Class dates and the final workload will be confirmed separately.

Stage 1: Foundations with retail data¶

Classes 1 to 4. You learn Tableau and the core habits of careful analysis using one retail dataset.

Class Business question You will produce
1 What makes a business question answerable? Tableau orientation and your first chart. A question, its population and a first evidence view.
2 What does a retail row represent? Data types, keys and preparation. A grain statement and a quality checklist.
3 Which retail segment deserves a closer look? Distinct orders, Sales, Profit, average order value and profit ratio. A checked KPI worksheet and a short debugging explanation.
4 How do trends and returns change the decision? Monthly growth and dashboard design. A retail dashboard and a recommendation.

Stage 2: One department at a time¶

Classes 5 to 8. Each class is a new business area. Support is gradually reduced so you work more independently.

Class Business question You will produce
5 Finance: where is spending above plan? A reconciled budget-versus-actual view.
6 Operations: where do deliveries miss their promise? A service view that includes still-pending shipments.
7 Marketing: which campaigns need a closer look? A campaign comparison with notes on denominators.
8 HR: how is the workforce changing? A synthetic workforce snapshot and a fair comparison.

Stage 3: Bring it together¶

Classes 9 to 12. You combine evidence from several departments into one executive recommendation.

Class Business question You will produce
9 Which departmental findings agree or conflict? A five-domain evidence register and a briefing proposal.
10 What decision should executives make? A draft briefing with traceable evidence.
11 Does the recommendation survive scrutiny? A validation log and a revised briefing.
12 What action and follow-up measure should leaders approve? The final executive briefing and a portfolio reflection.

What you will be able to do¶

By the end of the course you should be able to:

  1. Turn a stakeholder's concern into a clear question with a population, a period and a decision.
  2. Identify what a row means, the keys, data types, units and missing records, and document your preparation.
  3. Calculate metrics with the right aggregation and the right denominator.
  4. Check results with reconciliations, small manual checks and alternative explanations.
  5. Build readable Tableau views and dashboards that make the important comparison obvious.
  6. Recommend an action while explaining limitations, trade-offs and how success will be measured.

Practice time¶

How much time to plan outside class

The recommended essential route is about 19 hours of practice in total:

  • 60 to 90 minutes in most weeks
  • about 2 hours in project weeks

Optional deeper practice brings this to about 25 hours. Both figures are estimates that will be tested in a pilot before the schedule is fixed.

How you will be assessed¶

Your portfolio includes evidence from all five business areas.

Your final briefing combines findings from at least three of them. The exact submission schedule and presentation format will be shared before assessment begins.

Feedback rewards correct reasoning and careful checking, not only attractive charts.

Extra practice topics¶

Alongside the five business areas, selected datasets from the Tableau sample-data catalog give you choices in sports, technology, government and more.

These use the same question-to-recommendation workflow and fit into your existing practice time or optional extensions. Each dataset keeps its own real dates, units and context.
