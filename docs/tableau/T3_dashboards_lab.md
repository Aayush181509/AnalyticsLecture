## The business decision

The commercial lead from Session 14 is back:

> *"Next week I present our plans to the leadership team. I need one page that shows how 2025 went against 2024, and whether anything should worry us before we expand. They will want to click a region and see its numbers. Make it impossible to misunderstand."*

The decision is **where to focus next year** and **what to check before expanding**. The page must answer it in a minute, and it must stay correct when someone clicks.

!!! note "About the data"

    **Superstore** is a sample published by Tableau about a **fictitious** company. Sales and Profit are in the **file's own monetary units**. The file does not state a currency, so we never call them dollars or any other currency. The returns list says only **which orders** appear on it: no dates, amounts or reasons.

    **marketing-v1** (Stretch only) is **synthetic** data generated by the course: seven paid campaigns, every day of 2025, amounts in neutral **currency units**. Attributed revenue is a synthetic single-campaign allocation; it does not prove a campaign **caused** a sale.

## Setup: open your T2 workbook

### Step 1: Save a copy

1. Open **Tableau Desktop Public Edition** and open `T2_lab_YourName.twbx`.
2. Choose **File → Save As** and save it as a **Tableau Packaged Workbook (*.twbx)** named `T3_lab_YourName`.

!!! tip "No T2 workbook?"

    Follow [Set up the Superstore data source](T1_fundamentals_lab.md#set-up-the-superstore-data-source) in the T1 lab first. Then create the fields in Step 3 below, and save as `T3_lab_YourName`.

### Step 2: Check the data source

1. Click the **Data Source** tab at the bottom left.
2. Check that **Orders** and **Returns** are on the canvas with a relationship line between them.
3. Check that the **Filters** area at the top right shows **1** filter: **Order Date** from 1 January 2023 to 31 December 2025.

### Step 3: Check your fields

Go to any sheet and look in the **Data** pane. You need these fields from T1 and T2, with exactly these names:

| Field | Formula | Made in |
|---|---|---|
| `Returned Order` | `NOT ISNULL([Order ID (Returns)])` | T1 setup |
| `Distinct Orders` | `COUNTD([Order ID])` | T1 C1 |
| `Profit Ratio` | `SUM([Profit]) / SUM([Sales])` | T2 C1 |

Create any field that is missing with **Analysis → Create Calculated Field...**.

### Step 4: Add the year fields

The tiles show **2025** with a comparison to **2024**, so they need both years in the same sheet ([T3 lecture, Part 3](T3_dashboards_lecture.md)). Create these calculated fields. Check that each says **The calculation is valid** before you click **OK**.

| Field | Formula | Format |
|---|---|---|
| `Sales 2025` | `SUM(IF YEAR([Order Date]) = 2025 THEN [Sales] END)` | Number, 0 decimals, thousands separator |
| `Sales 2024` | `SUM(IF YEAR([Order Date]) = 2024 THEN [Sales] END)` | Number, 0 decimals, thousands separator |
| `Sales Growth` | `[Sales 2025] / [Sales 2024] - 1` | Percentage, 1 decimal |
| `Orders 2025` | `COUNTD(IF YEAR([Order Date]) = 2025 THEN [Order ID] END)` | Number, 0 decimals, thousands separator |
| `Profit Ratio 2025` | `SUM(IF YEAR([Order Date]) = 2025 THEN [Profit] END) / [Sales 2025]` | Percentage, 1 decimal |
| `Returned Orders 2025` | `COUNTD(IF YEAR([Order Date]) = 2025 AND [Returned Order] THEN [Order ID] END)` | Number, 0 decimals |
| `Returned Share 2025` | `[Returned Orders 2025] / [Orders 2025]` | Percentage, 1 decimal |

To set a format: in the Data pane, right-click the field and choose **Default Properties → Number Format...**. For a number with no decimals, choose **Number (Custom)**, set **Decimal places** to `0` and tick **Include thousands separators**.

In plain words:

- `Sales 2025` keeps the Sales of order lines placed in 2025, then adds them up.
- `Orders 2025` counts each 2025 order once. Lines from other years give a blank, and `COUNTD` ignores blanks.
- `Returned Share 2025` is a **ratio of counts**: orders on the returns list divided by all orders, both for 2025. It is not an average of anything.

!!! warning "Call it the share on the returns list"

    `Returned Share 2025` is the **share of orders on the returns list**, not a "return rate". The list says nothing about how many items came back or what was refunded.

Save your workbook (**File → Save**).

## Core tasks

### C1 (Core): Build the sheets

You need six sheets: four tiles, one trend and one region chart. Build each on its own sheet; the dashboard comes in C2.

#### Part A: The first KPI tile

1. Create a new sheet and name it `Tile Sales`.
2. Drag **Sales 2025** onto **Text** on the Marks card. The view shows one number.
3. Drag **Sales Growth** onto **Text** as well. Both pills now sit on the Marks card.
4. Click **Text** on the Marks card, then click the **...** button. The **Edit Label** window opens.
5. Delete what is there and write three lines. Use **Insert** to add the fields:

    ```
    Sales, 2025
    <AGG(Sales 2025)>
    <AGG(Sales Growth)> vs 2024
    ```

6. Select the middle line and set a large font size, for example `24`, in bold. Set the other two lines to about `10`. Click **OK**.
7. Right-click the sheet title and choose **Hide Title**. The first line of the tile is its label.

!!! tip "If the pills show SUM instead of AGG"

    `Sales 2025` already contains its aggregation, so Tableau shows it as **AGG(Sales 2025)**. If you see **SUM(Sales)** instead, you dragged the wrong field.

#### Part B: Three more tiles

Duplicate `Tile Sales` three times (right-click the tab → **Duplicate**). In each copy, drag the old pills off the Marks card, drag the new fields onto **Text**, and edit the text:

| Sheet | Fields on Text | Label text |
|---|---|---|
| `Tile Orders` | `Orders 2025` | `Orders, 2025` / value / `distinct orders` |
| `Tile Profit Ratio` | `Profit Ratio 2025` | `Profit ratio, 2025` / value / `Profit ÷ Sales` |
| `Tile Returns` | `Returned Share 2025`, `Returned Orders 2025`, `Orders 2025` | `Orders on returns list, 2025` / share / `<Returned Orders 2025> of <Orders 2025> orders` |

!!! tip "A comparison on every tile"

    The Sales tile compares with 2024. If you have time at the end, give the Orders and Profit Ratio tiles a 2024 comparison too: create `Orders 2024` and `Profit Ratio 2024` in the same way as the 2025 fields, and add them to the tile text.

#### Part C: The trend

The Session 14 trend drew 2025 against 2024, month by month, so the reader can see whether growth was steady or came from one lucky month.

1. Create a new sheet named `Trend`.
2. Drag **Order Date** to **Columns**. Right-click the pill and choose the **first** **Month** in the menu (the blue one, with an example such as *May*). The axis now shows the twelve months of the year.
3. Drag **Sales** to **Rows**.
4. Drag **Order Date** onto **Color** on the Marks card. Tableau shows **YEAR(Order Date)**: one line per year.
5. Drag **Order Date** to the **Filters** shelf, choose **Years**, click **Next**, tick **2024** and **2025** only, and click **OK**.
6. Set the colours: click **Color** → **Edit Colors...**. Give **2024** a grey and **2025** a blue. Click **OK**.
7. Make the years readable without colour: drag **Order Date** onto **Label** (it shows **YEAR(Order Date)**). Click **Label**, tick **Show mark labels**, and under **Marks to Label** choose **Line Ends**.
8. Check the vertical axis starts at zero: right-click it, choose **Edit Axis...**, and make sure **Include zero** is ticked.
9. Title the sheet, for now descriptively: `Monthly Sales, 2025 against 2024 (file's own monetary units)`. You rewrite it as a message title in C4.

!!! note "Is this year filter safe?"

    Yes. This sheet has no table calculation, so removing 2023 changes nothing for 2024 and 2025. Compare the growth chart in [T2 S2](T2_calculations_kpis_lab.md), where filtering months broke `LOOKUP`.

#### Part D: The region chart

1. Create a new sheet named `Returns by region`.
2. Drag **Region** to **Rows** and **Returned Share 2025** to **Columns**.
3. Click the **Sort descending** icon in the toolbar, so the longest bar is at the top.
4. Drag **Returned Share 2025** onto **Label**, so each bar shows its value.
5. Drag **Returned Orders 2025** and **Orders 2025** onto **Tooltip**. Hover over a bar to check both appear.
6. Drag **Region** onto **Color**. Click **Color** → **Edit Colors...**. Give **every** region the same grey, then give the **one** region your message is about an orange. Click **OK**.
7. Title the sheet: `Share of 2025 orders on the returns list, by region`.

Look at the chart and decide which region your message is about. Write one sentence in your log saying why.

Save your workbook.

### C2 (Core): Lay out the dashboard

Before you start, sketch the page on paper: headline, a row of four tiles, the trend across the full width, the region chart, and the scope note at the bottom.

1. Choose **Dashboard → New Dashboard**. Name the tab `Decision dashboard`.
2. In the **Dashboard** pane on the left, under **Size**, choose **Fixed size** and **Generic Desktop** (or another fixed size your facilitator gives). Do this **first**.
3. Under **Objects**, check that **Tiled** is selected at the bottom.
4. Drag a **Horizontal** object from **Objects** to the top of the page. This container holds the tiles.
5. Drag the four tile sheets, one by one, **into** the horizontal container. Watch for the grey shaded area: it shows where the sheet will land. Order: Sales, Orders, Profit Ratio, Returns.
6. Click the container's edge to select it. Open its drop-down menu (the small arrow at the top right) and choose **Distribute Contents Evenly**.
7. Drag `Trend` below the tiles, across the full width.
8. Drag `Returns by region` below the trend.
9. Drag a **Text** object to the bottom of the page and write the scope note:

    ```
    Scope: Superstore orders placed in 2024 and 2025 (fictitious sample published by Tableau).
    Sales in the file's own monetary units. Profit ratio = Profit ÷ Sales. Returns list = which
    orders appear on it; no dates, amounts or reasons. Click a region to filter the tiles and trend;
    click it again to clear. Patterns describe what happened, not why.
    ```

10. Choose **Dashboard → Show Title**. Leave the default title for now; you write the headline in C4.

!!! tip "Remove what you do not need"

    Tableau adds legends to the dashboard automatically, for example the **YEAR(Order Date)** colour legend. The trend already labels its lines, and the region chart labels its bars, so you can remove these legends: select one and click the **x** at its top right.

!!! warning "Sheet moved to the wrong place?"

    Press **Ctrl + Z** (Windows) or **Cmd + Z** (Mac) to undo, and drag again. The **Layout** pane, next to the Dashboard pane, shows the tree of containers and lets you select an object that is hard to click.

Save your workbook.

### C3 (Core): Add a filter action, then reconcile

The leadership team wants to click a region and see its numbers.

#### Part A: Add the action

1. Choose **Dashboard → Actions...** and click **Add Action → Filter...**.
2. Name the action `Filter by region`.
3. Under **Source Sheets**, choose `Decision dashboard` in the drop-down and tick **only** `Returns by region`.
4. Under **Run action on**, choose **Select**.
5. Under **Target Sheets**, tick the four tile sheets and `Trend`. Untick `Returns by region`, so the reader can still see and click every region.
6. Under **Clearing the selection will**, choose **Show all values**.
7. Click **OK**, then **OK** again.

Click one region's bar on the dashboard. The tiles and the trend now show that region only. Click the same bar again to clear the selection.

#### Part B: Reconcile across regions

A tile that changes when you click must still add up. Click each region in turn and copy the tile values into a table in your log:

| Region | Sales 2025 | Orders 2025 | Returned Orders 2025 | Returned Share 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (first region) | | | | |
| (second region) | | | | |
| (third region) | | | | |
| (fourth region) | | | | |
| **Added up** | | | | (do not add) |
| **No region selected** | | | | |

Then check, and write each answer in your log:

- [ ] Do the four regions' **Sales 2025** add up to the value with no region selected? Why must they?
- [ ] Do the four regions' **Orders 2025** add up to the value with no region selected? If they do, what does that tell you about orders and regions? Compare with categories in [T2 C1 Part C](T2_calculations_kpis_lab.md).
- [ ] Do the four regions' **Returned Orders 2025** add up?
- [ ] Divide the added-up Returned Orders by the added-up Orders with a calculator. Does it equal the **Returned Share 2025** tile with no region selected?
- [ ] Now take the simple average of the four regions' shares. Is it the same? Which one is the company's share, and why? *Hint: [T2 lecture, Part 2](T2_calculations_kpis_lecture.md).*

#### Part C: Reconcile the tiles with the trend

1. Right-click the `Trend` sheet tab and choose **Duplicate as Crosstab**. Tableau creates a table of Sales by month and year.
2. Choose **Analysis → Totals → Show Column Grand Totals** (if the totals appear across instead of down, try **Show Row Grand Totals**).
3. The **2025** total must equal the **Sales 2025** tile with no region selected. Divide the 2025 total by the 2024 total and subtract 1. The result must equal the **Sales Growth** on the tile.
4. Count the months in which 2025 Sales are higher than in the same month of 2024. Write the count in your log; you use it in your headline.

!!! note "Small rounding differences"

    The tile rounds to whole units. If your totals differ from it only by rounding, that is not an error.

Save your workbook.

### C4 (Core): Headline, accessibility, phone layout and export

#### Part A: Message titles

1. Rewrite the `Trend` title as a **message title**: what should the reader notice about 2025 against 2024? Use the month count from C3 Part C. The title must be true for the data shown.
2. Rewrite the `Returns by region` title as a message title that names the region your orange bar is about.
3. Double-click the dashboard title and write your **headline**: your big idea in one sentence, with a point of view and what is at stake. Look back at your big idea from the Session 14 lab.

**Test the titles after a click.** Click each region in turn. Is the trend title still true for every region? If not, rewrite it so it is true for every selection, or say in the title that it describes all regions. The region chart is not filtered, so its title does not change.

#### Part B: Accessibility check

Go through your dashboard with this list. Write **yes**, **no** or **fixed** next to each line in your log.

- [ ] The headline and the tiles can be read without looking at any chart.
- [ ] No more than five views (the four tiles count as one).
- [ ] Each colour means one thing everywhere: grey = context, blue = 2025, orange = needs attention.
- [ ] Nothing is shown **only** by colour: the years are written at the line ends, and the orange bar has its value and a title that names it.
- [ ] No red–green pair is the only difference between two things.
- [ ] Text is large enough to read when the page is shown at half size.
- [ ] The trend's axis starts at zero and the bars start at zero.
- [ ] The scope note gives period, units, source, how to use the click, and the main limit.

**See it without colour.** Export the dashboard (Part D) and view the PDF in greyscale (print preview in black and white, or your phone's greyscale setting). Can you still find the region your title is about? How?

#### Part C: Phone layout

1. At the top of the Dashboard pane, click **Device Preview**.
2. Under **Device type**, choose **Phone**, then click **Add Phone Layout**.
3. In the phone layout, keep the headline, the tiles and the region chart. Remove or move the other objects so nothing is too small to read.
4. Click **Default** at the top of the Dashboard pane to go back to the desktop layout.

Write one sentence in your log: which view did you leave out of the phone layout, and why?

#### Part D: Export and save

1. Click any selected bar again so that **no region is selected**.
2. With the `Decision dashboard` tab open, choose **File → Print to PDF...**. Under **Range**, choose **Active Sheet**, and click **OK**. Save as `T3_lab_YourName.pdf`.
3. Open the PDF and check that the headline, tiles, titles and scope note are all readable.
4. Save your workbook (**File → Save**).

## Stretch tasks

### S1 (Stretch): A dashboard for the marketing case

In the Session 14 lab, the marketing case asked: *which channels deserve more budget next year, and which need a review?* Build a one-page Tableau dashboard that answers it. Re-read the [Session 12 lecture](../lectures/12_marketing_campaigns_lecture.md) first if you need to.

#### Step 1: Download the file

The marketing-v1 files are on this course's public GitHub page, the same place the Python notebooks download them from.

1. Open the [marketing-v1 folder on GitHub](https://github.com/Aayush181509/AnalyticsLecture/tree/main/data/synthetic/marketing-v1).
2. Click **README.md** and read the field list and rules. Note the unit of the money columns.
3. Go back and click **marketing_daily.csv**. Click the **Download raw file** button (a downward arrow, at the top right of the file view).
4. Save it in your course data folder. Keep the name `marketing_daily.csv`.

!!! tip "If the download button is missing"

    Open the [raw file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Aayush181509/AnalyticsLecture/main/data/synthetic/marketing-v1/marketing_daily.csv) instead. Your browser shows the text. Save it with **File → Save Page As...** (or **Ctrl + S** / **Cmd + S**) and keep the `.csv` ending.

#### Step 2: Connect and check

1. In your T3 workbook, choose **Data → New Data Source**.
2. Under **To a File**, click **Text file** and open `marketing_daily.csv`.
3. Look at the field types in the preview. **date** must show a calendar icon. If it shows **Abc**, click the icon and choose **Date**.
4. Go to a new sheet. In the Data pane, check that the marketing data source is selected at the top. The fields may appear with tidied names, for example **Attributed Revenue** for `attributed_revenue`. Use the names **you** see in the formulas below.
5. **Check the grain.** README says one row per day and campaign. Drag the count field (**marketing_daily.csv (Count)**) onto **Text**. It must equal 365 days × 7 campaigns. Then drag **campaign_id** onto **Text**, right-click its pill and choose **Measure → Count (Distinct)**: it must give 7. Do the same with **date**: it must give 365.

#### Step 3: Create the measures

README says rates are **ratios of sums** and must never be averaged. Create:

| Field | Formula | Format |
|---|---|---|
| `ROAS` | `SUM([attributed_revenue]) / SUM([spend])` | Number, 2 decimals |
| `CPA` | `SUM([spend]) / SUM([conversions])` | Number, 2 decimals |
| `Review Case` | `[spend] = 0 AND [conversions] > 0` | (a True/False field) |

In plain words: **ROAS** is attributed revenue per unit of spend; **CPA** is spend per conversion. A row with no spend but some conversions is a **review case** (README): it cannot have a ROAS of its own. Put **Review Case** on **Rows** and the count field on **Text**, and write the number of review rows in your log.

#### Step 4: Build the sheets

| Sheet | How to build it |
|---|---|
| Four tiles: `M Tile Spend`, `M Tile Revenue`, `M Tile ROAS`, `M Tile CPA` | As in C1 Part A, with **SUM(spend)**, **SUM(attributed_revenue)**, **ROAS** and **CPA**. Third line: the unit (currency units, revenue per unit of spend, spend per conversion) |
| `ROAS by channel` | **channel** on Rows, **ROAS** on Columns, sorted descending, ROAS on Label. Add **SUM(spend)** to **Tooltip**, then right-click it and choose **Quick Table Calculation → Percent of Total** to show each channel's share of spend |
| `CPA by channel` | The same, with **CPA** |
| `M Trend` | Continuous **MONTH(date)** on Columns (the second **Month**, green). Drag **spend** to Rows, then drag **attributed_revenue** onto the same axis so Tableau shows **Measure Values** with **Measure Names** on Color. Label the line ends |

Use the same colour rule as the Core dashboard: grey for context and one highlight colour for the channel your message is about, in **both** channel charts.

#### Step 5: Lay out and add the actions

1. Create a dashboard named `Marketing dashboard`, **Fixed size**, laid out like the Core dashboard: headline, tiles, trend, then the two channel charts side by side in a horizontal container, then a scope note. The scope note gives the period (2025), the unit (currency units), the source (synthetic marketing-v1), what attributed revenue does **not** show, and the review cases.
2. Add a **filter action**: source `ROAS by channel`, run on **Select**, targets the four tiles and `M Trend`, clearing shows all values.
3. Add a **highlight action**: **Dashboard → Actions... → Add Action → Highlight...**. Source sheets: `ROAS by channel` and `CPA by channel`. Target sheets: the same two. Run on **Select**. Under **Target Highlighting**, choose **Selected Fields** and tick **channel**.

Click a channel in the ROAS chart. The tiles and trend filter to that channel, and the same channel lights up in the CPA chart.

#### Step 6: Reconcile and read

- [ ] Click each channel and write its **Spend** tile. Do the five add up to the Spend tile with no channel selected?
- [ ] Divide total attributed revenue by total spend with a calculator. Does it equal the **ROAS** tile with no selection?
- [ ] Take the simple average of the five channel ROAS values. Is it the same as the overall ROAS? Which is right, and why?
- [ ] Find the channel with the highest ROAS. What is its share of spend? Would you move most of the budget there? Use the CPA chart and the scope note in your answer.

Write a message headline for the marketing dashboard. It must not claim that a channel **caused** revenue. Export the dashboard as a PDF too: `T3_lab_YourName_marketing.pdf`.

!!! warning "Synthetic does not mean publishable yet"

    The marketing data is synthetic, but you do not need to publish this dashboard. T4 covers what is safe to publish to Tableau Public.

For each problem, write one row in your log:

| Problem | Which rule or check it breaks | Your fix |
|---|---|---|
| | | |

Then answer:

### X2 (Challenge): Prove two of the problems in Tableau

Use your own `Decision dashboard` workbook to show that two of the problems change the numbers, not only the look.

**Problem A: the average of shares.**

1. Duplicate `Returns by region` and name the copy `Average of shares`.
2. Create a calculated field named `Avg of Region Shares`:

    ```
    WINDOW_AVG([Returned Share 2025])
    ```

    In plain words: the simple average of the share bars in the view, each region counting the same.

3. Drag **Avg of Region Shares** onto **Label**. Right-click it and choose **Compute Using → Region**.
4. Compare the label with the **Returned Share 2025** tile with no region selected. Which is larger? Use the Orders 2025 values from C3 Part B to explain why, in two sentences: which regions have the most orders, and what weight does the simple average give them?

**Problem B: a filter that applies to one sheet.**

1. Go to the `Trend` sheet. Drag **Region** to the **Filters** shelf, keep all regions, and click **OK**. Right-click the pill and choose **Show Filter**.
2. On the dashboard, click the **Trend** sheet, open its drop-down menu and choose **Filters → Region** to add the filter control to the page.
3. Pick one region in the control. Which views change? Write down what the tiles say and what the trend says. Do they describe the same selection?
4. Open the filter control's drop-down menu and choose **Apply to Worksheets → Selected Worksheets...**. Tick the tiles and `Trend`. Pick the region again. Now which views change?
5. Remove the filter control and the Region pill from the Filters shelf, so your submitted dashboard uses only the filter action.

**Optional, Problem C: the truncated axis.** On a duplicate of the trend or region chart, right-click the value axis, choose **Edit Axis...** and untick **Include zero**. Write one sentence on how the picture changes. Then delete the duplicate.
